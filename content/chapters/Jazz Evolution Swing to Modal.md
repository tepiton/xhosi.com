Five exemplars per era, in recording order. Read top to bottom and the music moves from dance-band swing, to small-group virtuosity, to blues-and-gospel-rooted hard bop, to scale-based modal improvisation.

### 1. Swing (c. 1935-1945)

Big bands, riff-based arrangements, a steady four-to-the-bar pulse built for dancing.

| Year | Track | Artist | Release | What to listen for |
|------|-------|--------|---------|--------------------|
| 1937 | [Sing, Sing, Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing,_Sing,_Sing_(With_a_Swing)) | [Benny Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Goodman) | 78 rpm single | Gene Krupa's tom-tom drive; the swing era at full volume |
| 1937 | [One O'Clock Jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_O%27Clock_Jump) | [Count Basie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Basie) | 78 rpm single | Kansas City riffs and a light, bouncing rhythm section |
| 1939 | [Body and Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_and_Soul_(1930_song)) | [Coleman Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleman_Hawkins) | 78 rpm single | Harmonic improvisation that points toward bebop |
| 1940 | [Cotton Tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Tail) | [Duke Ellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington) | 78 rpm single | "I Got Rhythm" changes at a fast tempo; Ben Webster's tenor |
| 1941 | [Take the "A" Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_the_%22A%22_Train) | Duke Ellington | 78 rpm single | Billy Strayhorn's writing; polished swing at its peak |

### 2. Bebop (c. 1944-1955)

Small groups, fast tempos, dense chord substitutions. Music for listening, not dancing.

| Year | Track | Artist | Release | What to listen for |
|------|-------|--------|---------|--------------------|
| 1945 | [Salt Peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Peanuts) | [Dizzy Gillespie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizzy_Gillespie) with [Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker) | 78 rpm single | The bebop manifesto: angular unison lines, breakneck tempo |
| 1945 | Ko-Ko | Charlie Parker | 78 rpm single (Savoy) | Parker improvising over "Cherokee" changes at extreme speed |
| 1947 | ['Round Midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%27Round_Midnight_(song)) | [Thelonious Monk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelonious_Monk) | Blue Note 78 | Monk's dissonant voicings and use of space |
| 1947 | [Manteca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manteca_(song)) | Dizzy Gillespie | 78 rpm single | Afro-Cuban rhythm meets bebop (Chano Pozo on conga) |
| 1951 | Un Poco Loco | [Bud Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bud_Powell) | [The Amazing Bud Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Amazing_Bud_Powell) | Bebop piano at its most intense and inventive |

### 3. Hard Bop (c. 1954-1965)

Bebop's vocabulary, reconnected to blues, gospel, and R&B. Grittier, more soulful, groove-forward.

| Year | Track | Artist | Release | What to listen for |
|------|-------|--------|---------|--------------------|
| 1955 | The Preacher | [Horace Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Silver) | [Horace Silver and the Jazz Messengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Silver_and_the_Jazz_Messengers) | Gospel-flavored melody; a founding hard bop record |
| 1956 | St. Thomas | [Sonny Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonny_Rollins) | [Saxophone Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone_Colossus) | Calypso groove; Rollins's thematic improvising |
| 1957 | Blue Train | [John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coltrane) | [Blue Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Train_(album)) | Minor blues, three-horn front line, Blue Note sound |
| 1958 | Moanin' | [Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Blakey) | [Moanin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin%27_(album)) | Call-and-response straight from the church |
| 1963 | The Sidewinder | [Lee Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Morgan) | [The Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sidewinder) | Boogaloo groove; hard bop's commercial high point |

### 4. Modal (c. 1958-1968)

Solos built on scales (modes) held for long stretches instead of rapidly changing chords. More open, spacious, and exploratory.

| Year | Track | Artist | Release | What to listen for |
|------|-------|--------|---------|--------------------|
| 1958 | Milestones | [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis) | [Milestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)) | Miles's first modal composition; long sections on one scale |
| 1959 | So What | Miles Davis | [Kind of Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kind_of_Blue) | The defining modal tune: two scales, 32 bars, lots of air |
| 1960 | My Favorite Things | John Coltrane | [My Favorite Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Favorite_Things_(album)) | A show tune stretched into a hypnotic modal vamp on soprano sax |
| 1965 | Maiden Voyage | [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock) | [Maiden Voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Voyage_(album)) | Suspended chords floating over a gentle ostinato |
| 1967 | Nefertiti | Miles Davis | [Nefertiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefertiti_(Miles_Davis_album)) | Horns repeat the melody while the rhythm section improvises |

### The Progression at a Glance

| Era | Ensemble | Tempo/Feel | Harmony | Purpose |
|-----|----------|------------|---------|---------|
| Swing | Big band | Steady, danceable | Simple-to-moderate chord changes | Dancing |
| Bebop | Small combo | Very fast | Dense, rapid chord substitutions | Listening, virtuosity |
| Hard Bop | Small combo | Medium, grooving | Bebop plus blues and gospel | Listening, feeling |
| Modal | Small combo | Open, varied | Few chords, long scales | Exploration |
