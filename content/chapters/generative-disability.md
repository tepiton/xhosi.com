[Doc Pomus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doc_Pomus) wrote "Save the Last Dance for Me" after watching everyone else dance at his own wedding — polio kept him in a wheelchair, so he sent his bride out to dance with his brother, his father, his friends, and wrote the lyric later on the back of the invitation. He also co-wrote [|A Teenager in Love](https://youtu.be/1fgnEDi7bq0?si=6jh90eCdQNFnIDfW), the defining song of ordinary teenage romantic swooning, having never had an ordinary teenage romance himself.

The pattern: someone makes the clearest possible artifact of an experience they were denied. Not writing *despite* the exclusion, and not quite writing *about* it either — the exclusion is the generative mechanism. The absence produces the specificity.

Surfaced 2026-07-24 while building the Pomus person note. Checked the vault for anything that already held this shape — nothing did. The nearest thing found was [[father-summary-gpt-5.5]], Philip's father-writing, which circles a related but distinct shape: a person built in relation to an absence, not a person who *made something* out of the absence. Worth distinguishing, not merging.

Ein kol chadash tachat hashemesh — nothing new under the sun, but new to this vault. Flagging so temoa/zeitgeist/resonance can find it later if the pattern recurs.

## Where else this might show up

- Artists who depict a physical or social world they can't fully access
- Writers who theorize connection/intimacy from a position of relative isolation
- Any case where the constraint isn't overcome by the work but is legible *in* the work
