## tell me about the rohiu

The Rohingya are a predominantly Muslim ethnic minority group originating from Myanmar's Rakhine State. They have faced decades of systemic discrimination, violence, and persecution in Myanmar, where they are not recognized as an official ethnic group and have been denied citizenship since 1982. This has rendered them the world's largest stateless population, severely restricting their access to education, healthcare, and employment opportunities.[2][3]

The most significant crisis involving the Rohingya began in August 2017 when the Myanmar military launched a brutal crackdown following attacks by the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army on police posts. This led to widespread violence, including reports of mass killings, sexual violence, and arson against Rohingya villages. As a result, over 700,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh within weeks, joining thousands who had fled previous waves of violence.[1][3][5]

Today, nearly one million Rohingya live in refugee camps in Bangladesh's Cox's Bazar region, which is now the largest refugee settlement in the world. Conditions in these camps are challenging, with overcrowding and limited access to basic services like clean water, sanitation, and healthcare. The international community has described the Rohingya as one of the most persecuted minorities globally, and efforts continue to address their humanitarian needs and seek durable solutions for their statelessness and displacement.[3][4][1]
